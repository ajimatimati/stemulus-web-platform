What to Look For:
• PWM allows digital pins to simulate analog voltages by varying on-time vs off-time.
• Higher duty cycle delivers more average energy to LEDs and motor coils.
• NeoPixels receive serial data packets telling each pixel its exact RGB byte values.
Analog vs. PWM Trick

Digital pins only do ON or OFF. To get half-brightness, we turn the pin ON for half the time and OFF for half the time thousands of times a second. This is PWM!

The Duty Cycle

Duty cycle is the percentage of time a pulse stays HIGH. 0% is completely dark, 50% is gentle glow, and 100% is full blazing power.

Addressable NeoPixels

A NeoPixel strip has a tiny microchip inside every single LED. Using just one signal pin, code sends a train of Red, Green, and Blue bytes to light up any color.

Common Traps for this Lesson
Colour Without Contrast

Light grey text on white looks stylish but is hard to read. Ask the student to squint at the screen.

Too Many Fonts and Colours

Limit the design to two fonts and a small palette so the layout, not the decoration, does the talking.

Floating Input Pins

Unconnected digital inputs pick up static noise. Enable internal Pin.PULL_UP or Pin.PULL_DOWN resistors.

Rule of Two Questions (Zero Touch Typing)

Never take over the student's mouse or keyboard. Ask two progressive questions:

  1. "Look at the line right above the cursor: how does it differ from our example?"
  2. "If the computer reads left-to-right, what character did it encounter first?"
