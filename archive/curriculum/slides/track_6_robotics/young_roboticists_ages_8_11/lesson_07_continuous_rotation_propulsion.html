What to Look For:
• Differential drive robots control position using independent left and right wheel speeds.
• Opposing wheel velocities allow zero-radius turning on the spot.
• Wheel encoders or calibration trims are essential to compensate for real-world mechanical drift.
Continuous Servos

By disconnecting the internal physical stop and potentiometer, a servo transforms into a variable-speed bi-directional gearbox motor that spins continuously.

Differential Steering

Two independent wheels control everything: Both forward = Drive Straight. Both backward = Reverse. One forward and one backward = Spin in Place on a dime!

Dead Reckoning Calibration

Due to tire friction and battery level differences, two identical motors rarely spin at the exact same speed. Code calibration factors keep the robot driving in a true straight line.

Common Traps for this Lesson
Missing Common Ground (GND)

When using a separate battery pack for motors or servos, always connect battery GND to microcontroller GND so PWM signals have a 0V reference.

Powering Servos from 3.3V Logic Pin

Motors draw high stall current that causes CPU brownout resets. Power actuators from a dedicated supply rail.

Floating Input Pins

Unconnected digital inputs pick up static noise. Enable internal Pin.PULL_UP or Pin.PULL_DOWN resistors.

Rule of Two Questions (Zero Touch Typing)

Never take over the student's mouse or keyboard. Ask two progressive questions:

  1. "Look at the line right above the cursor: how does it differ from our example?"
  2. "If the computer reads left-to-right, what character did it encounter first?"
