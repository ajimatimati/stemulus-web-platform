What to Look For:
• Reflexion mimicks human writing: draft, critique, edit, finalize
• Evaluation rubrics give models objective standards to score against
• Iterative refinement consistently outperforms single-shot zero-shot generation
The Generator Agent

Drafts the narrative scene according to specific style requirements (Cyberpunk, Noir, Fantasy).

The Critic/Judge Agent

Evaluates the draft against a 4-point rubric (sensory detail, pacing, vocabulary, tone fidelity).

The Self-Correction Loop

Feeds rubric score and specific critiques back into the generator until threshold score >= 9/10.

def reflexion_loop(prompt, rubric, max_retries=3): draft = generator_model.generate(prompt) for attempt in range(max_retries): critique = judge_model.evaluate(draft, rubric) if critique.score >= 9.0: return draft # Threshold achieved! draft = generator_model.refine(draft, critique.feedback) return draft

Common Traps for this Lesson
Flex Rules on the Wrong Element

display: flex goes on the parent container. Setting it on the items does nothing to their spacing.

justify-content vs align-items

justify-content works along the main axis (row by default). align-items works across it. Students swap them often.

Vague Single-Sentence Prompts

Students write 2-word prompts. Remind them of the 3-part formula: Role, Task, Format Constraints.

Rule of Two Questions (Zero Touch Typing)

Never take over the student's mouse or keyboard. Ask two progressive questions:

  1. "Look at the line right above the cursor: how does it differ from our example?"
  2. "If the computer reads left-to-right, what character did it encounter first?"
